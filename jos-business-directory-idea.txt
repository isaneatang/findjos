THE IDEA
--------
A local discovery platform for Jos, Plateau State, Nigeria.

Business owners register their business (name, location, category, photos,
menu/services, hours, WhatsApp number). Residents search for a service or food
and find a match nearby. Starts Jos-only, deliberately.

Goal: increase online presence for local businesses that currently have none,
and make them findable to people who are actively looking to spend money.


WHY JOS IS AN ADVANTAGE, NOT A LIMITATION
-----------------------------------------
- Small enough to physically cover on foot. You can personally visit every
  business that matters. Nobody operating remotely from Lagos or Abuja can
  match that.
- Underserved. Google Maps coverage in Jos is thin, most businesses have no
  web presence at all, and discovery currently happens through WhatsApp
  groups, Facebook, and word of mouth.
- University of Jos means a large, dense, young, phone-native population with
  daily spending habits — the ideal starting user base.
- Winning a small city completely is worth far more than being invisible in a
  big one. The playbook then transfers to Kaduna, Bauchi, Makurdi, etc.


THE TWO TRAPS THAT KILL LOCAL DIRECTORIES
-----------------------------------------
1. CHICKEN AND EGG
   Consumers won't use a directory with 12 businesses in it. Businesses won't
   register for an app with no users. Most local directories die inside this
   loop.

2. NOBODY PAYS FOR A LISTING
   A business will happily register for free and never pay a naira. They pay
   for CUSTOMERS, not for being in a database. Monetisation must be tied to
   visible, provable results.

Both are solvable, but only if the product is designed around them from day
one. See "COLD START" and "MONETISATION" below.


STRATEGY 1: NARROW IT HARD — START WITH FOOD ONLY
-------------------------------------------------
"All businesses" sounds bigger but is much weaker.

  - A plumber directory gets used twice a year. People forget it exists
    between uses, then uninstall it.
  - Food gets used DAILY.

Daily use is what converts an app from "a thing I installed once" into a
habit. Habit is the only thing that survives the first six months.

Plan:
  Phase 1 — Food only. Restaurants, buka/mama-put, fast food, suya spots,
            small chops, cafes, drinks. Own this category completely.
  Phase 2 — Once people open the app by reflex, expand outward:
            barbers/salons, tailors, mechanics, phone repair, laundry,
            printing, plumbers/electricians, cleaners.

Do not launch with 20 categories and 4 businesses in each. Launch with 1
category and 200 businesses in it.


STRATEGY 2: COLD START — DON'T WAIT FOR REGISTRATIONS, GO GET THEM
------------------------------------------------------------------
Do not build a signup form and wait. Nobody will come.

Spend the first two weeks walking Jos with a phone. Photograph and record
150-200 food businesses yourself:
    name, exact location/landmark, category, what they sell, price range,
    phone/WhatsApp number, opening hours, 1-3 photos.

YOU are the content team for v1. This is unglamorous and it is the ENTIRE
MOAT. Any developer can clone the code in a weekend. Nobody else has walked
Jos.

This also completely changes the sales conversation:

  WEAK:   "Please register your business on my app."
  STRONG: "You're already listed. 300 people saw your listing last week.
           Want to be top of the 'rice' search for #5,000/month?"

Practical notes for the walk:
  - Ask permission, explain it's free, take the photos there and then.
  - Collect the owner's WhatsApp number — that's your future sales channel.
  - Log everything in a spreadsheet first; import to the database later.
  - Cover clusters: around Unijos, Terminus, Rayfield, Bukuru, Angwan Rukuba,
    British American Junction, Farin Gada, etc.


STRATEGY 3: PLATFORM DECISIONS (IMPORTANT — THESE ARE NIGERIA-SPECIFIC)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- SKIP iOS ENTIRELY FOR V1.
  iPhone share in Nigeria is small. Building for it would burn a third of the
  effort for a fraction of the users. Android-first, or rather web-first.

- BUILD A PWA (mobile web app), NOT AN APP-STORE APP.
  Reasons: data is expensive; cheap Android phones run out of storage; asking
  someone to download 40MB to find jollof is a hurdle you don't need; no Play
  Store review delays; you can share it as a plain WhatsApp link. Users can
  "Add to Home Screen" and it behaves like an app.

- BUILD FOR SLOW CONNECTIONS.
  Compress every image aggressively (WebP, lazy loading, thumbnails). If it's
  slow on 3G, it is dead. Test on a cheap Android phone, not on a laptop.

- WHATSAPP IS THE TRANSACTION LAYER — DO NOT REBUILD IT.
  No in-app chat. No in-app ordering. No payments in v1.
  Every listing gets a "Message on WhatsApp" button using a wa.me link with a
  prefilled message ("Hi, I saw you on JosFinder — do you have...?").
  Nigerian commerce already runs on WhatsApp. Go with the current.

- Consider low-end fallbacks later: a plain SMS or USSD lookup, or a WhatsApp
  bot, for users who won't install anything.


TECH STACK (chosen for a solo builder working with AI assistance)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Frontend/Backend : Next.js + TypeScript  (one language for everything)
  Database/Auth    : Supabase (Postgres, auth, file storage, generous free tier)
  Styling          : Tailwind CSS
  Hosting          : Vercel (free tier covers well past the first 1,000 users)
  Images           : Supabase storage + aggressive compression
  Maps             : skip real maps at first — use area/landmark text, which is
                     how people in Jos actually navigate anyway
  Payments (later) : Paystack or Flutterwave; expect bank transfer to dominate

If a real native app is ever needed, React/TypeScript knowledge carries
directly into Expo / React Native, so nothing is wasted.


MONETISATION — CHARGE FOR VISIBILITY, NOT FOR LISTINGS
------------------------------------------------------
Listings are free for everyone. Money comes from attention.

  FREE      — #0/month        Name, phone, location, category, 1 photo
  VERIFIED  — ~#3,000/month   Badge, photo gallery, full menu, hours, links
  PROMOTED  — ~#10,000/month  Top placement in search for their category

100 businesses at an average of #5,000/month = #500,000/month. In Jos that is
real money, and it's reachable within a year IF the consumer usage is genuine.

DO NOT take a commission on orders at the start. You cannot enforce it — buyer
and seller will simply move to WhatsApp and cut you out. Charge for the thing
you actually control: placement and visibility.

THE ONE FEATURE THAT MAKES RENEWALS HAPPEN:
Show every business their own numbers — views, clicks, WhatsApp taps, search
appearances. Nobody renews a subscription they can't see working.
"47 people tapped your number this month" is what makes #5,000 feel cheap.
Send it to them monthly on WhatsApp whether they log in or not.


V1 SCOPE — FOUR SCREENS, NOTHING MORE
-------------------------------------
1. SEARCH + BROWSE
   Search box, category chips, area filter. Fast. Works with no account.

2. LISTING PAGE
   Photos, name, category, what they sell + prices, hours, area/landmark,
   "Message on WhatsApp" button, "Call" button.

3. BUSINESS DASHBOARD
   Claim/edit listing, upload photos, update menu and hours, SEE YOUR STATS.

4. ADMIN (you)
   Add, approve, edit, and de-duplicate listings. Bulk import from spreadsheet.

EXPLICITLY NOT IN V1:
  - consumer accounts / login (adds friction, no benefit yet)
  - ratings and reviews (an empty ratings system looks broken — add once there
    is real traffic)
  - ordering, delivery, payments, in-app chat
  - maps and turn-by-turn directions
  - a native app

Target: ship v1 in about three weeks, then spend the time on the ground.


ROUGH FIRST 90 DAYS
-------------------
  Weeks 1-2   Walk Jos. 150-200 food listings collected into a spreadsheet.
              Talk to owners. Learn what they actually want.
  Weeks 3-5   Build v1. Import the listings. Test on a cheap Android phone.
  Week 6      Soft launch. Share the link in Unijos WhatsApp groups, Facebook
              groups for Jos, hostels, X/Twitter. No paid ads yet.
  Weeks 7-10  Fix what breaks. Watch what people actually search for — the
              empty searches tell you which businesses to go add next.
  Weeks 11-12 Start selling. Go back to the businesses with their real stats
              and sell Verified/Promoted. Target the first 10 paying.

Budget the time roughly 30% building, 70% on the ground. The bottleneck is
listings and users, not code.


HOW TO KNOW IF IT'S WORKING
---------------------------
Watch these, not vanity numbers:
  - Repeat visitors (are people coming back weekly? THE key metric)
  - WhatsApp button taps per listing (this is the "value delivered" number)
  - Searches that return zero results (tells you exactly what to add next)
  - Businesses asking to be added without being asked (organic pull = real)
  - Conversion from free to paid after showing stats


RISKS AND HONEST CONCERNS
-------------------------
- MARKETPLACE MODELS ARE SLOW TO MONETISE. This is a two-sided network; it
  typically takes a long ramp before meaningful revenue. If short-term income
  is the priority, this should not be the only thing being worked on.
- Facebook groups and WhatsApp groups are the real incumbent, and they're free
  and already have the users. The product must be meaningfully faster and
  better organised than scrolling a group.
- Jiji, Google Maps, and food-delivery apps may expand into Jos.
- Content decay: listings go stale (businesses close, prices change, numbers
  change). Needs an ongoing refresh process.
- Security/movement conditions in parts of Plateau can affect fieldwork —
  plan routes sensibly.
- Trust: people must believe the listings are accurate. One wrong phone number
  costs a user forever. Verify before publishing.

WHEN PICKING THIS BACK UP, START HERE
-------------------------------------
1. Do NOT open a code editor first. Go and talk to 10 food business owners in
   Jos. Ask: how do new customers find you today? Have you ever paid to be
   advertised? What did it cost, did it work?
2. Ask 10 students/young workers: how do you decide where to eat? Where do you
   look?
3. Only then decide whether to build. The answers will tell you whether this
   is real or just a nice idea.
